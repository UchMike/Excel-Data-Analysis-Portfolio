**Project Overview**

This project analyzes a heart disease dataset to explore how demographic factors, symptoms, and clinical measurements relate to observed heart disease prevalence. The goal is not to diagnose individuals, but to identify meaningful patterns, highlight associations, and support basic risk awareness through structured data analysis.

**Key Steps**
- **Dataset Documentation:** Reviewed all variables to understand definitions, scope, and limitations.
- **Data Preparation:** Cleaned categorical encodings, validated ranges, and engineered new features such as age groups, blood pressure and cholesterol categories, fasting blood sugar indicators, diabetes flags, and general risk markers.
- **Exploratory Analysis:** Used pivot tables to examine heart disease prevalence across age, sex, blood pressure, cholesterol levels, ECG results, chest pain type, and exercise‑induced angina.
- **Pattern Discovery:** Investigated how individual indicators—and combinations of indicators—relate to higher observed prevalence of heart disease.
- **Dashboard Creation:** Built an Excel dashboard summarizing prevalence, distributions, and key risk patterns for clear and accessible insight communication.

  **Findings**
-The analysis shows that several clinical indicators are strongly associated with heart disease, while others act as risk signals without consistently matching confirmed cases. These gaps highlight the difference between risk factors and actual outcomes and underscore the importance of cautious interpretation.

**File Structure**
-The Excel workbook is organized into several sheets, arranged in a logical sequence that guides the reader through the full analysis:
- **Project Overview** – Introduces the purpose, goals, and analytical approach.
- **Datascope and Structure**– Describes the dataset, variable definitions, scope, and structure.
- **Data Notes and Validation** – Documents cleaning steps, validation checks, and derived fields.
  **Findings** – Summarizes key analytical insights.
- **Conclusion** – Highlights major takeaways and limitations.
- **Dashboard** – Visual summary of prevalence patterns and risk indicators.
- **Pivot Tables** – All pivot‑table analyses used in the project.
- **Raw Dataset** – The original dataset used for all transformations.
  
**How to Use**
- Open the Excel file.
- Start with the Project Overview sheet for context.
- Review Datascope and Structure and Data Notes and Validation to understand the data foundation.

- View the Dashboard for a visual summary of insights.
    
**Limitations**
- The dataset does not represent a full clinical population.
- Indicators reflect associations, not diagnostic conclusions.
- Some variables may contain noise or incomplete encoding.
